<section class="research-editorial-section resonance-significance" aria-labelledby="resonance-significance-title">
  <header class="research-editorial-heading">
    <p class="research-section-index">02 / Project significance</p>
    <h2 id="resonance-significance-title">Rebuilding ritual presence in digital space</h2>
  </header>
  <div class="resonance-prose">
    <p lang="en"><em>Resonance</em> begins with a central question: when interaction is no longer organized around bodily co-presence, how can ritual recover affective weight and symbolic intensity in the “weightless” digital sphere?</p>
    <p lang="en">Rather than treating ritual as a visual theme, the project translates its social mechanisms into repeatable spatial and interaction principles: establish a legible center and boundary; use low-latency rhythmic feedback to support synchrony; anchor memory through a small set of strong symbols and replayable traces; and make participants mutually visible so they can move from spectators to co-performers. Developed through practice and examined across multiple public settings, these principles position the work as both an installation and a testable design method.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-editorial-section resonance-theory" aria-labelledby="resonance-theory-title">
  <header class="research-editorial-heading">
    <p class="research-section-index">03 / Theory translation</p>
    <h2 id="resonance-theory-title">From Collins’s Interaction Ritual Chains to an immersive design framework</h2>
  </header>
  <div class="resonance-prose">
    <p lang="en">Randall Collins’s Interaction Ritual Chain theory describes four ingredients of a successful ritual: bodily co-presence, a boundary to outsiders, mutual focus of attention, and a shared mood. These conditions can generate solidarity, emotional energy, shared symbols, and common standards. In digitally mediated settings, however, bodily co-presence and sensorimotor coupling are weakened or redistributed, making collective attention and shared feeling harder to sustain.</p>
    <p lang="en"><em>Resonance</em> treats the smallest perceivable cycle—participant action, system feedback, shared trace, and role shift—as a designable micro-ritual. The theory is translated into concrete spatial, interface, and interaction decisions, then connected to observable HCI indicators and methods of evaluation. The result is a framework that can be applied and tested through practice rather than remaining at the level of metaphor.</p>
  </div>

<section class="research-editorial-section resonance-architecture" aria-labelledby="resonance-architecture-title">
  <header class="research-editorial-heading">
    <p class="research-section-index">04 / Spatial architecture and apparatus</p>
    <h2 id="resonance-architecture-title">A musical gathering translated into an annular field</h2>
  </header>
  <div class="resonance-prose">
    <p lang="en">The spatial concept begins with <em>Hele Tu</em> (合乐图), traditionally attributed to Zhou Wenju of the Five Dynasties–Southern Tang period. The handscroll gathers musicians around a shared field of performance, making rhythm, reciprocal attention, and ensemble relations visible. <em>Resonance</em> does not reproduce the painting literally; it translates this cultural structure into an inward-facing arrangement organized around a common center.</p>
    <p lang="en">Within a darkened 4 × 2.5 × 3 metre room, a two-tier frame-drum apparatus is encircled by four digital screens. The upper mallets are system-driven and establish the pulse; the lower mallets respond to audience actions, creating a call-and-response between machine and participant. The apparatus also takes the Shanghai Museum’s Coiled-Dragon Frame Drum as a morphological reference, recasting the ritual instrument as a contemporary interactive interface. Live camera mapping places participants inside the surrounding imagery, reinforcing mutual visibility and the transition from spectator to co-performer.</p>
  </div>

<section class="research-editorial-section resonance-implementation" aria-labelledby="resonance-implementation-title">
  <header class="research-editorial-heading">
    <p class="research-section-index">05 / Media integration and technical implementation</p>
    <h2 id="resonance-implementation-title">One control system coordinates image, rhythm, sensing, and movement</h2>
  </header>
  <div class="resonance-prose">
    <p lang="en">TouchDesigner functions as the central orchestration layer, integrating Unreal Engine 5 visual output with Arduino-based sensing and mechanical actuation. Capacitive touch sensors register strikes, photoresistors detect occupied seats, and a camera captures live images for screen mapping. Servo-driven mallets, multichannel audio, and UE5 sequence changes return immediate audiovisual and kinetic feedback.</p>
    <p lang="en">The system coordinates waiting, entry, resonance, and closure within a single control graph. Keeping this loop legible and low-latency allows each action to be perceived not as an isolated trigger, but as part of a shared rhythmic structure.</p>
  </div>

<section class="research-editorial-section resonance-flow" aria-labelledby="resonance-flow-title">
  <header class="research-editorial-heading">
    <p class="research-section-index">06 / Interaction flow and ritual structure</p>
    <h2 id="resonance-flow-title">Four acts move the visitor from observer to co-performer</h2>
  </header>
  <div class="resonance-prose">
    <p lang="en">The experience unfolds as a continuous arc from perception to participation, collective intensification, and release. Each act coordinates spatial cues, sensing logic, tempo, and feedback so that the visitor’s changing role remains perceptible.</p>
  </div>
  <ol class="resonance-stage-list">
    <li>
      <p class="resonance-stage-index">Act I</p>
      <h3>Preludial attraction</h3>
      <p lang="en">The apparatus leads with a system-driven visual and rhythmic motif, establishing a clear pulse and focal center before the visitor enters.</p>
      <figure class="resonance-figure"><a href="/images/projects/resonance/flow-stage-01.webp" aria-label="Open Act I interaction diagram at full size"><img src="/images/projects/resonance/flow-stage-01.webp" alt="Act I diagram showing preludial attraction before a visitor enters" loading="lazy" /></a><figcaption>Act I · Preludial attraction</figcaption></figure>
    </li>
    <li>
      <p class="resonance-stage-index">Act II</p>
      <h3>Audience seating and virtual co-presence</h3>
      <p lang="en">Seat sensing changes the tempo while live camera mapping places the visitor within the surrounding imagery. Crossing the spatial boundary turns a passerby into a participant.</p>
      <figure class="resonance-figure"><a href="/images/projects/resonance/flow-stage-02.webp" aria-label="Open Act II interaction diagram at full size"><img src="/images/projects/resonance/flow-stage-02.webp" alt="Act II diagram showing a visitor seated within the sensing area" loading="lazy" /></a><figcaption>Act II · Audience seating and virtual co-presence</figcaption></figure>
    </li>
    <li>
      <p class="resonance-stage-index">Act III</p>
      <h3>Collective resonance</h3>
      <p lang="en">Drum strikes intensify music, image, projection, and mallet movement through low-latency call-and-response. Human and machine now share the performance loop.</p>
      <figure class="resonance-figure"><a href="/images/projects/resonance/flow-stage-03.webp" aria-label="Open Act III interaction diagram at full size"><img src="/images/projects/resonance/flow-stage-03.webp" alt="Act III diagram showing collective interaction with the installation" loading="lazy" /></a><figcaption>Act III · Collective resonance</figcaption></figure>
    </li>
    <li>
      <p class="resonance-stage-index">Act IV</p>
      <h3>Ritual closure</h3>
      <p lang="en">The tempo decrescendos, symbolic traces replay, and the participant leaves the interaction field. The system then returns to its waiting state for the next gathering.</p>
      <figure class="resonance-figure"><a href="/images/projects/resonance/flow-stage-04.webp" aria-label="Open Act IV interaction diagram at full size"><img src="/images/projects/resonance/flow-stage-04.webp" alt="Act IV diagram showing ritual closure as a visitor leaves" loading="lazy" /></a><figcaption>Act IV · Ritual closure</figcaption></figure>
    </li>
