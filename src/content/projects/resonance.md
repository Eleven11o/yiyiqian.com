<section class="research-editorial-section resonance-context" aria-labelledby="resonance-context-title">
  <header class="research-editorial-heading">
    <p class="research-section-index">02 / Research context</p>
    <h2 id="resonance-context-title">From bodily absence to shared presence</h2>
  </header>
  <div class="research-editorial-copy">
    <p>As digital interaction becomes increasingly detached from physical co-presence, ritual communication can lose its affective and symbolic depth. <em>Resonance</em> responds by treating collective rhythm, mutual attention, and embodied feedback as materials for interaction design.</p>
    <p>The project draws on Randall Collins’s Interaction Ritual Chain theory and on the musical gathering depicted in <em>Hele Tu</em>, traditionally attributed to Zhou Wenju. Rather than reproducing a historical ritual, it translates the social mechanisms of ritual into a contemporary participatory environment.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-editorial-section resonance-lineage" aria-labelledby="resonance-lineage-title">
  <header class="research-editorial-heading">
    <p class="research-section-index">Spatial prototype</p>
    <h2 id="resonance-lineage-title">From <em>Hele Tu</em> to an annular field of co-presence</h2>
  </header>
  <figure class="research-source-image">
    <a href="https://www.artic.edu/artworks/72380/united-by-music-%E5%90%88%E6%A8%82%E5%9C%96" rel="noreferrer">
      <img src="/images/projects/resonance/hele-tu.png" alt="Hele Tu handscroll showing musicians gathered around instruments in a long ceremonial ensemble" loading="lazy" />
    </a>
    <figcaption>
      <span><em>Hele Tu</em> (合乐图), traditionally attributed to Zhou Wenju, Five Dynasties · Southern Tang. Ink and color on silk, 41.9 × 184.2 cm.</span>
      <a href="https://www.artic.edu/artworks/72380/united-by-music-%E5%90%88%E6%A8%82%E5%9C%96" rel="noreferrer">Art Institute of Chicago ↗</a>
    </figcaption>
  </figure>
  <div class="research-editorial-copy">
    <p><em>Resonance</em> takes the handscroll as the visual and spatial prototype for its entire scene design. Its gathering of musicians around a shared performance field suggested an inward-facing arrangement organized by collective attention.</p>
    <p>The project does not reconstruct the painting literally. The ensemble is translated into a central frame drum, four surrounding image planes, and participant positions that make looking, listening, and rhythmic action mutually perceptible.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-question-panel" aria-labelledby="resonance-question-title">
  <p class="research-section-index">03 / Research question</p>
  <h2 id="resonance-question-title">How can co-presence, mutual focus, and rhythmic entrainment become actionable design strategies for restoring emotional energy and shared presence in digital space?</h2>
</section>

<section class="research-editorial-section" aria-labelledby="resonance-method-title">
  <header class="research-editorial-heading">
    <p class="research-section-index">04 / Method</p>
    <h2 id="resonance-method-title">Theory translated through practice</h2>
  </header>
  <ol class="research-method-steps">
    <li>
      <span>01</span>
      <h3>Theoretical translation</h3>
      <p>Extract the core ingredients of ritual interaction—co-presence, boundary, mutual focus, shared mood, and feedback—as potential design conditions.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <span>02</span>
      <h3>Comparative work analysis</h3>
      <p>Examine representative interactive artworks to identify recurring spatial, symbolic, and affective strategies aligned with Interaction Ritual Chain theory.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <span>03</span>
      <h3>Framework construction</h3>
      <p>Map ritual ingredients to design dimensions, concrete interaction strategies, HCI indicators, and methods of evaluation.</p>
    </li>
  </ol>
</section>

<section class="research-editorial-section resonance-framework" aria-labelledby="resonance-framework-title">
  <header class="research-editorial-heading">
    <p class="research-section-index">Design framework</p>
    <h2 id="resonance-framework-title">Five ritual ingredients become five design moves</h2>
  </header>
  <div class="research-framework-list">
    <article>
      <p>01</p>
      <h3>Group assembly</h3>
      <strong>Embodied convergence</strong>
      <span>A circular, inward-facing arrangement supports mutual visibility and joint participation.</span>
    </article>
    <article>
      <p>02</p>
      <h3>Boundary</h3>
      <strong>Situational framing</strong>
      <span>A darkened interaction zone, guided entry, and clear focal area distinguish the ritual space.</span>
    </article>
    <article>
      <p>03</p>
      <h3>Mutual focus</h3>
      <strong>Symbolic anchoring</strong>
      <span>The central drum and converging sound, light, and imagery orient attention toward a shared event.</span>
    </article>
    <article>
      <p>04</p>
      <h3>Shared mood</h3>
      <strong>Rhythmic entrainment</strong>
      <span>Low-latency action-to-feedback cycles and intensifying audiovisual rhythm align participant behavior.</span>
    </article>
    <article>
      <p>05</p>
      <h3>Feedback loop</h3>
      <strong>Collective response</strong>
      <span>Joint triggers, enhanced feedback, and visible success states connect individual action to group experience.</span>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-editorial-section resonance-system" aria-labelledby="resonance-system-title">
  <header class="research-editorial-heading">
    <p class="research-section-index">05 / Creative system</p>
    <h2 id="resonance-system-title">An annular space for call and response</h2>
  </header>
  <div class="research-editorial-copy">
    <p>A circular frame-drum apparatus stands at the center of four projection panels. Visitors trigger audiovisual responses through rhythmic gestures while robotic mallets maintain a system-led pulse. A live camera feed reprojects visitors onto the surrounding screens, shifting their role from observer to co-performer.</p>
    <p>Unreal Engine 5 provides the skeletal animation and spatial imagery; TouchDesigner organizes musical and visual states; Arduino sensing modules connect the drum surface, seat cushions, and mechanical actuation. The system keeps the response loop legible and immediate.</p>
  </div>
  <dl class="research-system-index">

<section class="research-editorial-section resonance-flow" aria-labelledby="resonance-flow-title">
  <header class="research-editorial-heading">
    <p class="research-section-index">Interaction flow</p>
    <h2 id="resonance-flow-title">Four stages change the visitor’s role</h2>
  </header>
  <ol class="research-flow-list">
    <li>
      <span>Stage 01</span>
      <h3>Preludial attraction</h3>
      <p>The installation performs a prelude. A passerby remains a bystander, watching the animated screens and mechanical pulse.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <span>Stage 02</span>
      <h3>Audience seating</h3>
      <p>Sitting on a cushion activates the sensing system. The visitor crosses the spatial boundary and becomes an audience participant.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <span>Stage 03</span>
      <h3>Collective resonance</h3>
      <p>Striking the drum intensifies music, animation, projection, and mallet motion. The participant becomes a performer within the loop.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <span>Stage 04</span>
      <h3>Ritual closure</h3>
      <p>When the visitor leaves, the system returns to the prelude and resets the conditions for the next gathering.</p>
    </li>
  </ol>
